## Derived Rational Precision

### Counterexample

With nonnegative focus lead 0.9 s, a next-line onset 1 microsecond after zero produces an exact focus boundary of 0.5 microseconds, represented as `Time(1, 2_000_000)`. Commit `efb98b5` stores that fraction in an ordinary `ParagraphTime`. Its external-input validator rejects denominators above 1,000,000. The earlier minimum-duration calculation instead produced 1 ms, so the new clamp introduced a timestamp-rejection path. This is a source-derived failure, not a native test execution in Cloud.

Minimal synthetic TTML, with one second of synthetic audio:

```xml
<tt xmlns="http://www.w3.org/ns/ttml" xml:space="preserve"><body><div>
<p begin="0s" end="0.000001s">A</p>
<p begin="0.000001s" end="1s">B</p>
</div></body></tt>
```

`LocalPresentation(focusLead: Time(9, 10))` is sufficient; measured fill lead also reaches this case. Expected pre-follow-up failure: `ScreenError.invalid("Timestamp bounds")` during paragraph validation. TTML's existing six fractional digits for millisecond units additionally permit `0.000001ms`; with measured fill lead, the analogous exact boundary is 0.5 nanoseconds. Merely doubling the external denominator limit internally to 2,000,000 would miss that already accepted syntax.

### Minimal Correction

`ParagraphTime` retains its original public constructor, external numerator/denominator limits, and two-field Codable representation. A package-only initializer accepts a structurally valid `Time` generated by the focus-boundary calculation. A private runtime flag preserves that exact value when it exceeds external bounds. Explicit `CodingKeys` exclude the flag: decoding always restores the untrusted external-input state. Encoded internal fractions therefore do not carry authority to bypass validation when read back as external input.

`LocalScene` invokes this initializer only for a newly capped deadline and, when needed, its newly calculated earlier start. Supplied times and untouched intervals use the original initializer. No rounding, parser precision expansion, focus retiming, model/quality threshold change, or general public-input limit relaxation was introduced. Ordinary representable derived values keep the default flag and remain equal to their prior ordinary representations. The underlying `Time` constructor establishes a positive denominator, normalized fraction, and exclusion of `Int64.min`; the only production callers of the new initializer are the two boundary calculations in this closure.

The added input regression covers 1, 3, and 999 microseconds and the existing millisecond-unit precision. It checks scene construction, exact positive intervals ending at the unchanged focus event, complete presentation progress there, source-entry preservation, and unchanged rejection of excessive fractional-second precision. A separate core regression covers unchanged ordinary values, external invalid times, JSON provenance spoofing, the unchanged encoded keys, and loss of runtime authority when an internal value is decoded. These Swift tests have not been compiled or executed in Cloud.

## Remaining Counterexamples

### Empty Entries Do Not Use the Computed Deadline

Relevant paths: `LocalScene.swift`, deadline construction around lines 145–150 and empty-entry focus insertion around lines 130–132. A timestamped empty LRC entry receives an event at its exact timestamp, whereas the preceding paragraph calculates a deadline as if the empty entry were an ordinary led lyric line.

Minimal synthetic LRC, focus lead 0.9 s, fill lead zero, no gap insertion, one-second audio:

```text
[00:00.000]A
[00:00.001]
```

The actual release is at 0.001 s; the computed deadline is 0.0005 s. Before `efb98b5`, the presentation interval was `[0, 0.001]`, already within the actual focus boundary. The new clamp changes it to `[0, 0.0005]`. Thus unchanged intervals relative to the *computed* deadline do not establish unchanged intervals relative to the *actual* event schedule.

A less compressed Enhanced LRC example is `[00:00.000]<00:04.500>A<00:05.000>` followed by `[00:05.000]`. With the same presentation options and six-second audio, actual release is 5 s, but the calculated deadline is 4.1 s. The previous interval `[4.5, 4.501]` becomes `[4.099, 4.1]` even though it already finished before actual departure. Original source timing remains intact. A subsequent nonempty line can also have a led focus before the empty entry's event, so replacing the deadline with the immediate empty entry's timestamp alone is insufficient in general.

This event-schedule mismatch predates the patch; unnecessary advancement of these formerly bounded intervals is a consequence of applying the stronger clamp to it. Resolving it requires deriving the deadline from the actual compiled focus/release/gap schedule while preserving that schedule. No special-case deadline change was applied without a complete schedule reproduction.

### Smoothing Can Continue Fill Beyond the Numerical End

Relevant paths: `LyricsScreenMac/Paragraph.swift`, `smoothedFront` and the `render` branch that replaces per-span progress, and `LyricsScreenCore/Presentation.swift`, `WordMotion.measured`.

Use a paragraph beginning at zero and ending at 5.1 s, containing a single span at 4.42–4.52 s, followed by a paragraph beginning at 5.1 s. With measured focus lead 0.9 s, fill lead 0.22 s, and measured word motion, the corrected interval is `[4.199, 4.2]`. The presentation snapshot reports progress 1 at the 4.2 s deadline. The renderer then replaces that progress with a centered moving average of width 0.12 s.

For a synthetic unit of visual advance, the exact average at the deadline is `(0.0005 + 0.06) / 0.12 = 121/240`, approximately 0.50417. The full average reaches 1 only when the entire averaging window is past the final knot, at 4.26 s. This is an analytical result of the source formula, not a measured pixel or native-reference comparison. It demonstrates why snapshot endpoint assertions and frame repeatability alone do not prove completed visible fill at focus departure.

This renderer behavior is pre-existing and remains uncorrected. Changing smoothing globally would affect ordinary spans and fitted appearance; changing only clamped spans requires an explicit way to preserve the distinction through rendering. No smoothing parameter or renderer endpoint policy was changed.

### Independent Clamping Can Reverse Segment Starts

Use the same 5.1 s next-line onset, zero fill lead, and source spans `A: [4.1995, 4.2002]` and `B: [4.2002, 4.3]` inside a paragraph beginning at zero. These supplied spans are positive, ordered, and nonoverlapping. The corrected presentation intervals become `A: [4.1995, 4.2]` and `B: [4.199, 4.2]`.

At 4.19925 s, direct per-span evaluation gives A progress zero and B progress 0.25. The source UTF-16 ranges and times remain unchanged, but the drawing start order has reversed. With word motion, `frontKnots` are appended in source order and the smoothing lookup assumes temporal ordering; the resulting knot times are not ordered. Exact native pixels remain unverified. For measured fill lead, add 0.22 s to both source span intervals to produce the same presentation values.

The existing tests exercise one timed span per paragraph and do not cover this case. Preserving a preceding span that already ends at the deadline leaves no positive, nonoverlapping interval for later spans before that deadline. Allocation among multiple late spans therefore needs a deliberate contract for overlap or compression of preceding presentation intervals. No allocation policy was invented, and no source range, text, order, or source timestamp was modified.

### Gaps, Explicit Release, and the Final Line

The next-line deadline is not necessarily the first event that removes current focus. A TTML paragraph `[0, 0.0005]`, followed by another at 3 s, with fill lead zero and a minimum gap of 1 s, loses focus to the gap at 0.0005 s. Its 1 ms minimum presentation interval remains `[0, 0.001]`, because the computed next-line deadline is 2.1 s. An explicit release with `holdsFinishedLine == false` can produce the same earlier departure even without a gap.

For a final paragraph `[0.9995, 1]` with one-second audio and fill lead zero, no next-line deadline is constructed. Its presentation end remains 1.0005 s, beyond its source/audio end and beyond its explicit release when enabled. These paths are unchanged by both fill commits. The existing gap test uses a comfortably bounded 0.9–1 s span and therefore does not test this minimum-duration case. No new final-line, gap, or hold policy was introduced.

## Preserved Properties and Qualification

For a continuous sequence of nonempty entries with nonnegative focus lead, the next paragraph's capped lead uses the same pair of onsets as the deadline calculation. Its candidate focus lies after the current onset; the prior-focus clamp cannot make it earlier. That ordinary next-paragraph path matches the calculation. Empty entries and earlier release/gap events require separate treatment as described above.

For any positive computed deadline, the numeric clamp guarantees `0 <= begin < end <= deadline`. If the previously computed end is already at or before the deadline, the closure leaves both interval values unchanged. No source `LyricEntry` or `TimedSegment` is mutated. Event construction, source paragraph intervals, parser code, TTML export, and visible text are unchanged.
