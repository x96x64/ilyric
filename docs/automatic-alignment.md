# iLyric Automatic Singing Alignment Experiment

## Decision and Scope

**Go for a bounded, reviewed offline preparation prototype; no-go for unattended synchronization or completion of the mandatory product requirement.** Two candidates execute on Apple Silicon with supplied text and audio. TIFA is promising for accepted Japanese excerpts and improves some English onset failures, but neither candidate satisfies the predefined accuracy and coverage criteria. Full-song alignment, robust mismatch detection, and a distributable commercial model path remain unresolved.

The architecture is **untimed local text and audio → isolated optional Python inference → reviewable timing artifact → bounded TTML → existing Swift renderer**. No production renderer, parser, project schema, typography, motion, appearance, composition, or audio-encoding implementation changes. Project versions 1–3 and existing LRC/enhanced-LRC/TTML behavior remain intact. No provider lookup, transcription replacement, automatic acquisition, translation, material, symbol, or delivery refinement is introduced.

The empirical interface reference remains iPhone 16, reported iOS 27.0.1, exact build **Unknown**, with its archived settings unchanged. This experiment measures acoustic alignment, not native Music visual timing or fidelity. Historical reports remain unchanged; the new priority supersedes their earlier sequencing recommendations without rewriting them.

This experiment does not attribute every residual to one of these causes.

Only trusted pinned TIFA source is supported; its Python code is executed locally. No proprietary Apple assets or fonts are involved. No model or third-party implementation source is committed. ## Corpus and Protocol

The [protocol](alignment-data/v1/protocol.json) was written before inference. Proposed line-boundary acceptance targets are median absolute error at most 100 ms, p95 at most 250 ms, at most 5% of boundaries exceeding 500 ms, and no unmatched lines. Operational targets are warm runtime below ten times audio duration and peak RSS below 4 GiB. These are provisional product-engineering targets, not scientific standards or native highlight tolerances. Results are reported separately for onsets and offsets; p95 uses linear interpolation of the ordered errors. Small sample counts and unquantified annotator disagreement limit generalization.

The [numerical record](alignment-data/v1/measurements.json) contains neutral case IDs, source/excerpt hashes, counts, errors, and performance. Corpus labels are external human references, not independently reannotated ground truth. Japanese morphology, note boundaries, phones, morae, and written characters are not interchangeable.

Training independence cannot therefore be guaranteed. “Held out” means excluded from this experiment's development decisions; it does not certify absence from pretraining. An initial corpus-preparation defect included following, unsupplied words in two-second English trailing margins. Those [initial results](alignment-data/v1/incomplete-context-diagnostic.json) remain as mismatch diagnostics, not the principal accuracy estimate. The corrected rule ends context at the earlier of two seconds after the final supplied line or the midpoint to the next annotated line. It is fixed from annotations, without model fitting. EN02 has no gap: its final offset coincides with the excerpt endpoint and is excluded from aggregate offset acceptance metrics. Annotation-defined excerpts make the test easier than unknown full-song segmentation and must not be described as full-song validation.

## Prototype and Timing Semantics

[Commands and preparation contract](alignment-commands.md) document the experimental invocation. UTF-8 source text is retained with its hash. Blank lines separate paragraphs; nonblank physical lines within a paragraph are explicit display breaks. LF/CRLF and an optional BOM are handled only as file syntax, with original text retained. Punctuation, spaces, normalization-sensitive sequences, and displayed text are not rewritten. Limits are 64 KiB, 64 paragraphs, four explicit lines and fewer than 500 UTF-16 units per paragraph, 1,500 retained alignment characters, 3,000 acoustic units, and 60 seconds per complete recording. Longer audio is rejected, never silently truncated or divided using guessed lyric boundaries.

Alignment-side lowercase and punctuation/whitespace filtering retains a map to original UTF-16 ranges. It performs no Unicode normalization, accent stripping, or transliteration. The English CTC baseline accepts ASCII letters and contractions, while retaining the original display. Japanese uses MeCab/UniDic readings and TIFA pronunciation alternatives; unidic-lite is explicitly selected to prevent automatic full-dictionary download. Supplied written text remains authoritative. Model semantic units must concatenate to the normalized source exactly and may not cross explicit source lines. A missing long-vowel character or unsupported reading produces an unresolved result rather than a rewritten lyric. Unsupported symbols, including emoji, fail before alignment; existing render-side grapheme/glyph checks remain unchanged.

Audio preparation reads a separate mono 48-kHz PCM16 copy through FFmpeg; the CTC baseline additionally resamples that copy to 16 kHz. No separation, denoising, transcription replacement, or global synchronization offset is fitted. Original input hashes are checked. The existing renderer receives the supplied recording, not the model-analysis copy. No changes are made to AVFoundation decoding, sample-rate/channel conversion, AAC priming, or output scheduling.

The CTC decoder retains blank states and the required blank between repeated labels. It reports acoustic-grid intervals at 20 ms. TIFA raw integer frame intervals are captured at 10 ms before its TextGrid writer can omit, extend, or repair skipped states. Zero-width/skipped units and text mismatch remain unresolved. Phone diagnostics are not exported as character timing. Forced paths can still assign plausible-looking intervals to incorrect text; complete coverage and model scores are not proof of correctness.

The JSON result contains source text/hash, paragraph structure, audio identity/duration, normalized range maps, raw units, line estimates, model/configuration hashes, dependency versions, preprocessing, diagnostic indicators, unresolved regions, and review history. Scores are not calibrated probabilities. Original estimates have an integrity digest; corrections are separate, with notes and manual provenance. Validation checks coverage, ordering, positive intervals, bounds, overlaps, identity, and review history. A correction can be reused without loading a model.

Review is mandatory before TTML export. Unresolved output requires explicit corrections rather than automatic acceptance. Line estimates are aggregated into paragraph intervals; internal line gaps remain inside paragraph focus, while gaps between paragraphs retain explicit no-focus intervals. TTML uses integer microseconds serialized as exact six-place decimals, with no output-frame rounding. This is storage precision, not microsecond acoustic accuracy. The sidecar retains estimated/corrected provenance; TTML is a derived rendering input, and the renderer's “supplied” classification refers to file timing, not verified acoustic origin. Ordinary LRC would lose explicit ends, while fine-grained enhanced LRC would overstate the present qualification.

## Measured Accuracy and Failure Cases

No model parameters, offsets, or per-recording corrections were fitted for the following raw comparisons. All errors are native audio-time errors in milliseconds, separate from video/container synchronization.

| Held-Out Candidate and Language | Line Onset Median / p95 | Line Offset Median / p95 | Coverage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| English speech CTC | 129.907 / 2,454.335 | 135.828 / 240.119 | 6 onsets; 5 uncensored offsets; no missing lines |
| English TIFA | 127.890 / 339.371 | 325.727 / 865.918 | 6 onsets; 5 uncensored offsets; no missing lines |
| Japanese TIFA | 13.946 / 17.456 | 6.848 / 9.868 | 3 accepted utterances; 1 additional held-out utterance unresolved |

English CTC's worst held-out onset is 3,205.780 ms, with 1/6 onsets above 500 ms. TIFA's worst held-out onset is 345.828 ms, but its worst uncensored offset is 995.940 ms, with 1/5 offsets above 500 ms. TIFA is not uniformly superior. On the development English excerpt it also has a 608.572-ms onset residual. Both fail the acceptance rule.

English word correspondence is exact only after the documented punctuation/case mapping. Held-out CTC word onset median/p95 is 90.624/307.500 ms; TIFA is 82.495/336.788 ms. Offset median/p95 is 107.631/239.421 ms for CTC and 81.379/736.762 ms for TIFA, excluding the censored endpoint. Large tails remain despite favorable medians. These diagnostic word intervals are retained for analysis, not qualified for automatic fine-grained export.

Japanese JA02 fails normalized text coverage because the G2P result drops a written long-vowel character; JA06 fails on an unsupported kana reading. Accepted Japanese estimates do not compensate for those failures: total coverage is four of six, including three of four held-out utterances. Phone outputs and manual phone labels differ in segmentation, particularly repeated/sustained vowels, so no approximate phone pairing or phone-error claim is made. Reliable Japanese word, mora, syllable, or display-character synchronization is not established.

The incomplete-text diagnostic shows that both models can absorb unsupplied following vocals into the final supplied text without reporting missing coverage. This exposes an unresolved mismatch-detection limitation. Repeated occurrences retain separate text IDs, but detection of extra sung repetitions, omitted verses, overlapping singers, and long instrumental sections is not qualified. Vocal separation was not added: the evidence does not yet isolate accompaniment as the cause of the failures.

## Performance, Rendering, and Verification

Measurements used Apple M4, 16 GiB, macOS 27.0.1, CPU inference with four PyTorch threads, Python 3.12, Swift 6.4/macOS SDK 27, and FFmpeg 8.1.2. CTC corrected-case process times were 2.679–3.739 seconds; inference including model initialization was 1.629–2.717 seconds; peak RSS was 1,173–1,336 MiB. TIFA processes took approximately 13.7–18.8 seconds, including a rejected input; successful model/inference stages took approximately 15.3–17.1 seconds; peak RSS was approximately 1,045–1,101 MiB for accepted attempts. Weight construction/load was approximately 0.02–0.18 seconds; imports, G2P, local caches, and framework setup are outside that narrow initialization counter. These short-process measurements do not establish steady-state batch throughput or acceleration performance. No GPU claim is made.

The approved evaluation ceiling was 2 GB downloaded and 6 GB stored. Assets and wheels were installed into an ignored isolated environment, with no global Python or system-package modifications. The runtime explicitly requires local assets and blocks Python network connection attempts; inference was also executed under the network-restricted sandbox. ONNX telemetry is disabled before session creation. No model retrieval occurs during rendering.

Real EN03 and JA03 experiments began with untimed text and singing audio, produced estimates, and then applied the independent human reference boundaries as recorded corrections. This demonstrates reuse of a corrected artifact without rerunning inference; it does not turn corrected agreement into model accuracy. The existing renderer exported 863 frames/14.383333 seconds and 786 frames/13.1 seconds respectively, at 1080×1920, 60 fps, H.264, limited-range Rec.709, and mono 48-kHz AAC. All video presentation timestamps passed. Supplied-audio correlations were 0.999413 and 0.999341; best lag was zero at a 16-sample search step. Real singing contains no synthetic audiovisual markers; the separate original synthetic export checks retain their marker validation.

Representative decoded stills were inspected. English two-line wrapping/focus and Japanese four-line wrapping are intact, with the established static background, inactive blur, original controls, and uniform `contain` margins. No new fine-grained progression is implied: preparation exports line-level timing. This was sampled visual inspection, not a new native fidelity or acoustic listening assessment. The [media record](alignment-data/v1/singing-export.json) separates technical export agreement from acoustic errors.

Public tests use original text and deterministic synthetic model output; they require no inference packages, weights, corpus, network, or subscriptions. They cover normalization/ranges, repeated phrases, gaps, skipped units, invalid bounds, exact conversion, corrections, provenance, CTC repeated-label paths, unavailable dependencies, and TTML preparation. Existing Swift tests retain random-order state/raw-raster checks and typography/motion/appearance nonregression. All 61 Swift tests and 86 Python tests passed, including 14 new alignment tests. A fresh public-only checkout repeated those suites, built the release targets, and exported the 601-frame synthetic preparation example without models or private media. Private validation explicitly reported unavailable inputs. Existing TTML export/refusal checks, reference-probe random access, renderer determinism, and the architecture-spike audiovisual regression passed. The fresh synthetic export took 22.004 seconds (27.313 frames/s); this isolated run does not establish a performance change. The [validation record](alignment-data/v1/validation.json) records these outcomes. Initial sandbox cache/media-service restrictions were resolved by using the established authorized local test/export workflow, without changing the Apple toolchain or renderer.

## Next Mandatory Gate

Continue automatic synchronization before deferred visual work. The smallest corrective experiment is to compare explicit local pronunciation overrides for the two rejected Japanese cases and a silence-aware endpoint treatment for the same English recordings, retaining untouched recordings for subsequent confirmation. Diagnose the observed endpoint/word residuals before introducing vocal separation. Any new score or rejection threshold needs negative examples with omitted and repeated text, not only matched examples.

Then test automatic coarse segmentation and bounded overlap/reconciliation on complete songs longer than one minute, with additional Japanese singers and musical styles and no reference-derived clip boundaries. The current prototype deliberately rejects that workload. Keep model licensing/redistribution review separate: commercial product use requires a suitably licensed singing model or explicit authorization. The mandatory requirement remains incomplete until accuracy, coverage, full-song operation, and correction burden have measured practical support. No new physical iPhone captures are required.
